# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A shift worker with two open claims and a stable but variable income, who can't pay the full amount at once.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Settle both claims through a plan she can afford and keep, using her phone in a few spare minutes.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** The moment the journey asks for her income or documents. She doesn't know why it's asking, whether the plan will be affordable, what happens if she misses a payment, whether both claims can be handled together, or whether accepting could make things worse. If she can't find the documents, she gives up and calls an agent. This matches the research themes on affordability, "why this information" and fear of consequences.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** - Phone call to an agent (Daniel)
  - Purpose: Get her unanswered questions resolved and complete the plan manually
  - Status: Documented in the case study

- Her bank app
  - Purpose: Work out what she can afford, since her shift income varies
  - Status: Assumption (to be validated)

- Calculator or notes app
  - Purpose: Build her own monthly figure, because the portal gives no affordability guidance
  - Status: Assumption (to be validated)

- Email inbox, paper files or employer payslip portal
  - Purpose: Hunt for the income documents the journey asks for
  - Status: Assumption (to be validated)

- Phone camera / file upload
  - Purpose: Photograph or upload documents, often in poor quality
  - Status: Assumption (to be validated)
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** - Step 1: Abandons the journey at the income or document request
  - She can't answer why it's asked, whether the plan is affordable, or what happens if she misses a payment
  - Nothing is saved, or only a partial entry

- Step 2: Works out affordability on her own
  - Uses her bank app, calculator or notes
  - Her shift income varies, so she has no reliable monthly figure (inferred)

- Step 3: Hunts for income documents
  - Searches her email inbox, paper files or employer payslip portal
  - Photographs or uploads them, often in poor quality (inferred)

- Step 4: Gives up and calls an agent
  - Often later, outside her shift hours, after a wait
  - She repeats identity, affordability and case details

- Step 5: The agent resolves it manually
  - Daniel re-runs checks because the digital attempt gave him little context
  - Usually one claim at a time, unless he spots the second claim
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** - The moment
  - Mara is on her phone in a few spare minutes and the journey asks for her income or a document
  - It gives no reason why, no sign of whether a plan will be affordable, and no word on what happens if she gets it wrong or misses a payment

- Why it feels broken
  - She is asked to hand over sensitive information before she has any reassurance
  - She can't tell whether accepting could make things worse
  - She doesn't know whether both claims can be handled together
  - Her shift income varies, so "monthly income" has no single honest answer
  - If she can't find the document right now, there is no way forward in the journey

- The result
  - She gives up and calls an agent
  - The journey fails at exactly the point where she needed guidance, not a form
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** - Documented behavior (case study, Mara persona)
  - She opens the portal on her phone with little time to spare
  - She is unsure whether a plan would be affordable, why she is asked about income, what happens if she misses a payment, whether both claims can be handled together, or whether accepting could make things worse
  - If the journey confuses her or asks for documents she can't easily find, she calls an agent

- Research themes (early qualitative research)
  - Consumers don't know what an affordable agreement looks like
  - Consumers don't understand why specific information is requested
  - Consumers fear the consequences of giving a wrong answer or missing a payment
  - Agents receive too little context when a digital journey is escalated

- Behavioral data (fictional baseline)
  - 31% of journey starters contact an agent within 7 days
  - 28% of uploaded income documents need clarification or review
  - Only 46% of started journeys end in an agreement
  - 19% of new agreements miss a payment within 60 days

- Why this counts as proof
  - These are behaviors (what consumers do), not opinions (what they say), which makes them the stronger signal
  - The calls and document reviews show people leaving the digital path at the income and document step
