Executive Summary

The platform is technically in place and shows no sign of instability, with the portal, payment processing and case-management backends all operating. The experience built on top of it is where health breaks down: fewer than 4 in 100 consumers reached by outreach end up with an agreement, and 19% of those agreements miss a payment within 60 days. Consumers are unsure what is affordable, why they are asked for information, and what a mistake will cost them, so friction moves from the portal to the phone and agents inherit unresolved cases without context.

Thematic Synthesis
1. Comprehension and Clarity

Consumers enter the journey without a reference point for what a reasonable plan looks like, and the purpose of the questions they are asked is not explained. Without that framing, requests for income details feel intrusive rather than helpful, and the outcome of the process stays opaque.

Consumers don't know what an affordable agreement looks like: High
Consumers don't understand why specific information is requested: High
Consumers get no confirmation of what happens after they submit information. They don't know if the request was received, when they'll hear back, or whether a plan is active, so they call to check: Medium
2. Fear and Trust

Consumers approach the journey defensively. They worry that a wrong answer or a missed payment will trigger harsh consequences, and that discourages them from committing or from being open about their situation. Consumers in hardship find no visible route that acknowledges their circumstances.

Consumers fear the consequences of giving a wrong answer or missing a payment: Critical
Consumers in hardship (job loss, illness, family change) see no clear way to signal their situation or reach a person, and feel the journey was designed for "standard" cases: Critical
3. Journey Friction and Drop-off

The funnel loses most consumers at two points. Of 100,000 consumers reached each month, 38% enter the portal and 22% of those start an instalment journey. Of the starters, 46% reach an agreement, which means more than half abandon mid-journey. Consumers who do start often still call an agent, with 31% making contact within 7 days, so the digital path frequently fails to resolve the case.

More than half of started journeys end without an agreement: High
Consumers who start the journey still contact an agent within 7 days: High
Few consumers move from outreach into the portal or start the instalment journey: High
Reminders, letters and the portal feel like separate processes rather than one journey: Medium
4. Fit to Real-Life Circumstances

The journey assumes a tidy financial situation. Consumers with variable income or several open claims find that the options don't match how their finances actually work, which makes any commitment feel risky. Document requests add to this when the evidence is hard to find or doesn't fit.

Consumers with irregular income, such as shift workers, can't tell which fixed monthly amount is safe, and feel the form asks for one number that doesn't exist: High
Consumers with multiple claims are unsure whether they can be handled together, and worry that settling one will leave another unresolved or escalating: High
Income documents are hard to locate or upload, and 28% need clarification or review afterwards: High
5. Plan Sustainability

Reaching an agreement doesn't mean the consumer can keep it. Nearly one in five new plans misses a payment within 60 days, which suggests many consumers commit without a reliable sense of what they can afford and are then left alone when things go wrong. Because the headline metric counts a plan as resolved the moment it is created, this experience is invisible in the current success measure.

19% of new agreements miss a payment within 60 days: Critical
Consumers agree to plans they later can't keep because the journey didn't help them judge affordability: High
Consumers who miss a payment have no clear way to adjust the plan, so a single miss feels like the start of escalation: High
Consumers get no reassurance or reminders between agreement and first payment: Medium
6. Agent Experience on Escalation

Agents receive escalated consumers with little context, so they repeat identity, affordability and case checks the consumer has already completed. This frustrates both parties and slows resolution, and it undercuts the efficiency case for self-service.

Agents receive too little context when a digital journey is escalated, including what the consumer entered, why a recommendation was made, and prior communication: High
Agents can't easily correct or override an incorrect system result, so they work around it manually and consumers wait longer: High
